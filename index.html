📘 Grade 8 Study Reviewer

A. Mass / Weight / Volume

  • Balance / Scale: Measures mass (amount of matter) in grams (g) or kilograms (kg).
  • Spring scale & Gravity: Measures weight (pull of gravity) in Newtons (N). Mass stays the same everywhere, while weight changes with gravity.
  • Graduated cylinder, Beaker & Flask: Measure liquid volume in milliliters (mL). A graduated cylinder is the most accurate; always read the bottom of the meniscus at eye level.

B. Functions of Apparatuses

  • Beaker: Holding, mixing, and heating approximate volumes of liquids.
  • Erlenmeyer flask: Swirling liquids smoothly without spilling thanks to its conical shape and narrow neck.
  • Test tube & Rack: Holding small-scale chemical reactions safely upright.
  • Funnel: Pouring liquids into narrow openings cleanly and supporting filter paper.
  • Stirring rod: Mixing chemicals safely without splashing.
  • Evaporating dish: Heating liquids so the solvent evaporates and leaves solid crystals behind.
  • Wire gauze & Tripod / Iron ring: Supporting glassware evenly over a Bunsen burner flame.

C. Portable Apparatuses to Identify

Graduated Cylinder, Erlenmeyer Flask, Beaker, Dropper / Pipette, Funnel, Test Tube & Rack, Stirring Rod, and Evaporating Dish.

Why Does It Matter & What Does the Law Say?

  • Safe working protects: (1) You, (2) Other lab workers, (3) Cleaners, (4) Visitors, and (5) Your work.
  • Health Safety at Work etc Act 1974: You must work safely, must not endanger others, and must not misuse safety equipment. Penalty: up to 2 years in prison and/or an unlimited fine.
  • Management of Health and Safety at Work Regs 1999 & COSHH Regs 2004: You must perform RISK ASSESSMENTS.
  • How to do a Risk Assessment: Determine hazards & evaluate risks → Use all relevant available data → Determine controls needed to minimise risks → Document the assessment → Agree it with your supervisor → Use those control measures.

Control Measures (In Exact Order of Preference 1 → 8)

  1. Use a less risky substance
  2. Use a safer form of that substance (e.g., solution instead of powder)
  3. Totally enclose the process (e.g., a glove-box)
  4. Partially enclose the process (e.g., with a fume cupboard)
  5. Ensure good general ventilation
  6. Safe systems of work
  7. Reduce exposure times, increase distance, reduce volumes
  8. Personal protective equipment (PPE — as a last resort for primary protection)

Protecting Yourself, Gloves, Hygiene & Hazards

  • Clothing & Gloves: No sandals or open shoes; tie long hair back; wear protective wear identified in risk assessment; keep lab coats fastened; pick chemical gloves suited to the substances used; remove gloves before using instruments, telephone, and leaving the lab.
  • Laboratory Hygiene: Never eat, drink, or smoke; never apply cosmetics; never touch face, mouth, or eyes; never suck pens or chew pencils; always wash hands before leaving and especially before eating.
  • General Hazards & Symbols: Fire, breakage of glassware, sharps, spillages, pressure equipment & gas cylinders, extremes of heat & cold, chemical hazards, biological hazards, radiation; Symbols: General Warning, Biohazard, Flammable, Explosive, Electrical Hazard, High Voltage, Toxic Material, Ionizing & Non-Ionizing Radiation, Low Temp, Hot Surface, UV Light, Oxidizing, Corrosive, Glassware Hazard, Carcinogen, Irritant.

Grade 8 DNA Replication Overview

  • Purpose: Makes an identical copy of DNA in the nucleus before a cell divides.
  • Base-Pairing Rule: Adenine (A) pairs with Thymine (T); Cytosine (C) pairs with Guanine (G).
  • Helicase: "Unzips" the DNA double helix by breaking hydrogen bonds between base pairs.
  • Primase: Adds a short RNA primer starting point so copying can begin.
  • DNA Polymerase: The "builder" enzyme that adds matching free nucleotides to build new DNA strands.
  • Ligase: The "gluer" enzyme that seals/joins DNA fragments together on the lagging strand.
  • Semi-Conservative Result: Produces two identical DNA molecules, each containing one old (original) strand and one newly built strand.

Central Dogma: DNA → mRNA → Protein

  • Transcription (in the nucleus): DNA code is copied into single-stranded messenger RNA (mRNA) by RNA Polymerase. In RNA, Uracil (U) replaces Thymine (T), so DNA A pairs with RNA U.
  • Translation (at the ribosome in the cytoplasm): The ribosome reads mRNA in 3-letter groups called codons. Transfer RNA (tRNA) brings matching amino acids using its anticodon, linking them together to form a protein chain.
